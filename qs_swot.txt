Quantity Surveying (success)
  Overall: positive (medium confidence)
  From what I found, Quantity Surveying is still a solid choice for Sri Lanka in 2031. You have live local vacancies, a clear degree-to-IQSSL/RICS path, and real overseas options in Abu Dhabi, the Middle East, Australia, and Southeast Asia. The main watch-out is that junior pay is modest and senior roles take time, experience, and sometimes a master’s degree. If you choose this, start building BOQ, measurement methods, tendering, and internship experience early.
  Where the opportunities are: overseas stronger. Local QS jobs exist, but the research shows stronger pay and broader demand in the Middle East, Australia, and Southeast Asia. The better opportunities look overseas, though RICS/IQSSL progression and experience are needed.
  Further study: helpful. A degree is the start, not the finish. RICS membership, IQSSL corporate membership, and possibly a master’s degree help you reach senior posts and work abroad, but they are not the only way into the field.
  Strengths:
    - Quantity Surveying has a clear professional ladder in Sri Lanka: a U.G.C.-recognized degree gets you in, and IQSSL-linked progression plus strong BOQ (bills of quantities) and measurement skills help you move up. [E007, E005]
    - There is still active local hiring for QS-related roles in Sri Lanka, so the 2031 intake should not be entering a dead market. [E001, E008]
    - Your degree can travel: Sri Lankan QSs are already working in Abu Dhabi and other overseas markets, and RICS membership improves that portability. [E005, E006]
  Weaknesses:
    - The early-money stage is not amazing: junior QS pay starts around LKR 60k-90k or LKR 70,000-120,000, while the better money usually comes only after several years. [E003, E006, E010]
    - Reaching top posts can be slow and demanding, because CIDA-level senior roles want a master’s or corporate membership and very long experience after graduation. [E007, E016]
    - The work itself is exposed to industry shocks: IQSSL research flags automation, robotics, and material shortages as real issues in Sri Lankan construction. [E011]
  Opportunities:
    - Further study and membership can open better jobs and overseas mobility, especially if you aim for chartered status rather than stopping at the basic degree. [E006, E007]
    - Specialising in value engineering, life cycle costing, or cost-overrun control could make you more useful as projects get more complex and tech-heavy. [E011]
    - If you build strong estimating and drafting skills, the field also gives you a route into overseas or freelance work, not just one local employer. [E005, E006]
  Threats:
    - Construction volatility can hit QS work hard, because shortage problems and rapid economic swings can slow projects and squeeze fees. [E011]
    - Routine cost and measurement work may face more automation pressure over time, so you will need stronger judgement and contract skills than just software use. [E011, E013]
    - Students who wait until graduation may fall behind, because the student discussion suggests practical exposure matters early in this field. [E009]

Quantity Surveying limitations:
  - The research does not show a QS-specific Sri Lanka employment rate or graduate unemployment rate.
  - Most salary figures come from non-official career sites, so exact pay is uncertain.
  - Community posts reflect a small group’s experience, so they mainly show student and practitioner concerns, not the whole market.
