Dental Surgery (success)
  Overall: positive (medium confidence)
  From what I found, Dental Surgery looks like a solid choice for Sri Lanka in 2031. Local demand is steady, and there are visible entry points, so you should be able to start. The main watch-out is that the best pay usually comes later through specialisation, private practice, or work abroad. My practical advice: choose this only if you are ready for long training, then build digital and specialist skills early.
  Where the opportunities are: overseas stronger. Local demand is steady, but the strongest upside in pay and scale appears overseas, especially where licensing pathways exist. Those gains come with exam, experience and registration hurdles.
  Further study: helpful. Postgraduate study is helpful rather than essential: you can start after BDS, but specialisation improves pay, status and access to better roles locally and abroad.
  Strengths:
    - You are entering a field with real local demand, not a niche one; Sri Lanka already has a large dental service base and continues to treat millions of patients. [E005, E007]
    - A first job looks reachable because entry-level posts and vacancies are visible in both public and private channels. [E001, E007, E014, E016]
    - The career can become financially stronger over time, especially if you build private practice or specialise. [E002, E009, E016]
  Weaknesses:
    - You will likely need further study or specialisation to reach the better-paid and more secure end of the field. [E009, E014, E016]
    - Entry pay is decent but not spectacular, and some feedback from people in the field suggests the early years can feel financially tight. [E002, E008, E015]
    - The work is physically and mentally demanding, with long hours, repetitive procedures, patient anxiety and burnout risk. [E008, E016]
  Opportunities:
    - Specialist training in areas like orthodontics, oral surgery, implants and cosmetic dentistry can open higher-value work and a stronger private market. [E009, E013, E016]
    - Overseas work is a real option if you are willing to clear licensing and experience hurdles, with pathways mentioned for the UK, Australia and Gulf markets. [E012, E016, E003]
    - Digital dentistry and new tools can give you an edge if you learn them early, especially in private practice. [E016, E017]
  Threats:
    - Competition may rise for the most attractive jobs and income streams, especially in private practice and specialist tracks where reputation matters. [E015, E016]
    - If you ignore digital skills, you could fall behind as clinics adopt CAD/CAM, scanners and AI-assisted workflows. [E016, E017]
    - Going abroad is not automatic; licensing, exams and clinical experience are clear barriers that can slow or derail migration plans. [E012, E016, E003]

Dental Surgery limitations:
  - The research does not show how hard it will be to secure the best entry posts in 2031.
  - Most salary figures come from non-official pages, so pay estimates are approximate.
  - Community posts reflect a small group's experience, not the whole market.
